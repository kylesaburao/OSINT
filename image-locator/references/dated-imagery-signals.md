# Dated imagery and public records

## Compare the right scene date

When a candidate's visible feature conflicts with current imagery or a known reconstruction, compare imagery from before and after the plausible capture window. If no window exists, log the scene change without using it to date the photo. Record each map layer's capture date or range and source, rather than a page access date. [Google Earth explains that imagery and 3D content may be composites from different dates](https://support.google.com/earth/answer/6327779). [Street View metadata](https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/streetview/metadata) may provide a year and month, a year alone, or no date; its panorama position is a comparison viewpoint, not the supplied photo's camera point. Check provider access, coverage and dates at use time. Weather, tide and traffic can support timing only if a credible capture interval and local observations make them discriminating; transient conditions do not replace fixed geometry.

## Visible vessel or aircraft

Use public historical AIS or ADS-B only if a **distinctive visible object** and credible time window permit a meaningful match. Compare position, direction, visible type or markings and timing, with uncertainty; record archive coverage and timestamp basis. The match locates or identifies the moving object, while fixed sightlines still locate the camera. Public records are OSINT using signal-derived data, with no interception or private signal access required; [ODNI defines OSINT and SIGINT separately](https://www.odni.gov/files/documents/USNI%202013%20Overview_web.pdf).

- **Vessel:** [IMO's AIS description](https://www.imo.org/en/ourwork/safety/pages/ais.aspx) lists broadcast identity, position, course and speed. [NOAA's AccessAIS](https://coast.noaa.gov/digitalcoast/tools/ais.html) is one historical source; verify its current availability and geographic and date coverage. [NOAA's AIS FAQ](https://coast.noaa.gov/data/marinecadastre/ais/faq.pdf) explains gaps and data filtering. [IMO guidance](https://wwwcdn.imo.org/localresources/en/KnowledgeCentre/IndexofIMOResolutions/AssemblyDocuments/A.1106%2829%29.pdf) and [USCG guidance](https://navcen.uscg.gov/ais-frequently-asked-questions) describe incomplete or erroneous reports and system limits.
- **Aircraft:** [FAA's ADS-B description](https://www.faa.gov/air_traffic/technology/equipadsb/capabilities/ins_outs) lists broadcast position, altitude and velocity. Check archive reception, equipage and any [FAA privacy program](https://www.faa.gov/air_traffic/technology/equipadsb/privacy) that affects public identification; the [FAA FAQ](https://www.faa.gov/air_traffic/technology/adsb/faq) describes system scope.

Missing tracks are weak negative evidence because equipment, reception, coverage, filtering and reporting can fail. An apparent match can be stale or erroneous; [IMO also documents falsified AIS broadcasts](https://www.imo.org/en/ourwork/legal/pages/fraudulent-registration-and-fraudulent-registries-of-ships.aspx). Require independent visual and temporal agreement and retain contradictions in the candidate record.

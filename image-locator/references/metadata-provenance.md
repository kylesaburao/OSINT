# Metadata and provenance

## Preserve distinct claims

Retain the original bytes, note acquisition URL or sender, and work on copies. Record a SHA-256 hash when reproducibility matters; log crops, rotations, enhancements, and transcodes. The [Berkeley Protocol, preservation and verification sections](https://humanrights.berkeley.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/Berkeley-Protocol.pdf) recommends preserving and documenting digital evidence and testing its source and content.

Inspect available EXIF, XMP, IPTC, container, and filesystem fields with a suitable reader such as ExifTool; `exiftool -a -G1 -s FILE` exposes duplicate tag names and their groups, which default output may suppress. On macOS `sips -g all` can expose HEIC properties. Keep `DateTimeOriginal`, offset/time zone, GPS, file creation or modification, upload, publication, and search-index dates in separate columns. A platform copy may strip or rewrite fields, and a transcode may describe the processing step. A missing field is not negative location evidence. See [ExifTool's metadata FAQ](https://exiftool.sourceforge.net/faq.html) for duplicate-tag behavior.

[IPTC defines Location Created as where the photo was taken and Location Shown as a place depicted](https://iptc.org/std/photometadata/specification/IPTC-PhotoMetadata-2023.2.html). Check which field a caption or software display actually uses. Treat embedded thumbnails and previews as separate images: compare their scene, orientation and dimensions with the primary image. Deliberately edited GPS, clock, camera model or orientation fields can appear internally consistent, so test them against fixed pixel features, lighting and independent dated sources. A disagreement can also come from time-zone conversion, mirroring, export, or a later edit; document the competing explanations before rejecting a tag.

When angular geometry could help, also record focal length, 35 mm equivalent focal length, digital zoom ratio, lens/camera identifier, image dimensions, orientation, and any embedded camera intrinsics or distortion calibration. [EXIF focal length is the actual lens focal length](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/gdiplus/-gdiplus-constant-property-item-descriptions); [35 mm equivalent is a field-of-view convention](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfoundation/avcapturedevice/nominalfocallengthin35mmfilm), not the physical focal length to combine with a small sensor's width. These values are claims about capture settings, not proof that the delivered pixels retain the uncropped camera view. Check whether the file was cropped, digitally zoomed, stabilized, stitched, mirrored, or distortion-corrected before applying them. Follow [camera geometry](map-geometry.md#calibrated-field-of-view) for the angular test.

## Content Credentials and decoding

If present, validate C2PA bindings, signature and manifest chain with a compatible validator and inspect **who** made each assertion and **which asset or edit** it covers. [C2PA 2.4](https://spec.c2pa.org/specifications/specifications/2.4/specs/C2PA_Specification.html) defines validation of association, formation and tamper evidence; its trust model leaves the truth of an assertion to the consumer. A valid signature therefore cannot promote a claimed GPS point to a verified camera site. Missing or invalid credentials alone do not locate or discredit the scene.

For HEIC or other layered formats, verify that the decoded image is the primary composite, with plausible dimensions, colors and all visible scene elements. A decoder may expose tiles or auxiliary depth or segmentation layers. Compare the result visually with a known viewer before using its pixels. If every tag is absent or rejected, continue with the visual and geometry references without a metadata prior.
